# Project Brief

## Context

Chevron Constantine Banking processes credit applications every day through several
channels. Management has noticed a set of recurring symptoms in the credit department:

- processing times that vary a lot from one file to the next;
- a share of applications routed to manual review;
- applications with missing or incomplete information;
- errors surfacing during the verification steps;
- a workload on the credit team that keeps growing without a clear explanation.

None of this is unusual for a credit process on its own. What is missing is a precise,
data-backed view of why it happens and where the process actually loses time.

## Problem

The credit team does not currently have a clear, evidence-based understanding of what
drives processing delays and where the friction points in the process sit. Decisions
about where to invest (better checks upfront, more automation, more staff, a different
routing logic) end up being based on anecdote rather than on a structured read of the
portfolio.

## Objective

Identify the main factors associated with credit processing delays, and turn that
analysis into concrete recommendations for making the process faster and more
transparent, without weakening the risk controls the process exists to enforce.

## Scope

**In scope:**

- application intake;
- information and document collection;
- verification checks;
- file analysis;
- decision (approve / reject / manual review);
- manual review;
- communication of the decision to the customer.

**Out of scope:**

- building an actual credit decision engine;
- granting real credit to anyone;
- using real customer or banking data of any kind;
- any production deployment.

This project is a fictional Business Analyst case study. It does not reproduce, and is
not based on, the internal credit process, scoring model, or policies of any real bank,
including Belfius.
